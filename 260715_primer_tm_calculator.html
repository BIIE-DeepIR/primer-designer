How this compares to SnapGene, IDT & the vendor tools

Same core engine. SnapGene, IDT, and this tool all use the unified SantaLucia (2004) nearest-neighbor parameters. Given identical inputs, Tm values agree to within rounding — verified here against an independent Biopython implementation to ≤0.06 °C.

Reproduce SnapGene: pick the SnapGene / Na⁺ 50 mM preset (50 mM Na⁺, 250 nM, no Mg). Reproduce IDT: same NN table + Owczarzy divalent correction — set your real Na⁺/Mg²⁺/dNTP.

Why KAPA needs its own mode: KAPA HiFi HotStart ReadyMix runs at higher salt than conventional mixes, so its optimal annealing temperature is higher than a standard 50 mM calculator predicts. Roche gives no Tm formula — they recommend an annealing gradient, which this tool lays out for you.

Primer → template mapping

The tool anchors on the primer's 3′ end and finds the longest region that base-pairs perfectly with the chosen strand. Anything 5′ of that (e.g. a sequencing adapter or overhang) is reported as a non-annealing tail. The annealing-region Tm is what governs round-1 annealing; the full-primer Tm is what matters once the adapter is incorporated in later rounds.

Mapping summary

Primer designer

Each primer is grown one base at a time from the anchor until its Tm is as close as possible to the target; length is quantized, so the closest achievable Tm per template is chosen. Giving a range for the anchor base lets the tool also slide the fixed end to minimize deviation. Anchor 5′ at base 1 with "from"="to"=1 reproduces "start at the first base, extend until target Tm".
With the G/C clamp on, candidates are tried in order: (1) ends in G/C and ≤3 G/C in the last 5 nt — used whenever its Tm is within the max deviation field of the target. (2) If that safe option doesn't exist, or its Tm misses the target by more than the max deviation, the ≤3-in-5 mispriming guard is relaxed and the closest-Tm clamped candidate is used instead (flagged clamp, GC-rich end) — i.e. Tm accuracy is allowed to win over the mispriming guard, but never over the clamp itself. (3) If no length can even end in G/C, it falls back to best-Tm-only (flagged no G/C clamp). Leave the max-deviation field blank or 0 to always keep the mispriming guard whenever a safe clamp is achievable at all, no matter the Tm cost.

Method, assumptions & verification

Nearest-neighbor Tm. Tm = ΔH°·1000 / (ΔS° + R·ln(k)) − 273.15, SantaLucia (2004) unified ΔH/ΔS parameters, terminal-initiation and self-complementarity/symmetry corrections. R = 1.987 cal·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹. For a non-self-complementary primer at per-strand concentration C, k = C/2 (i.e. CT/4); self-complementary, k = C. This matches the SnapGene/IDT/Biopython convention.

Salt correction. Monovalent: SantaLucia (1998) entropy term 0.368·(N−1)·ln[Na⁺], or Owczarzy (2004). Divalent: Owczarzy (2008) decision tree with free [Mg²⁺] ≈ [Mg²⁺]total − [dNTP]total (dNTPs chelate Mg), applied on 1/Tm with length- and GC-dependent terms.

Verification. Both salt paths were checked against an independent Biopython implementation of the identical models across a panel of real primers: SantaLucia path ≤0.06 °C, Owczarzy/KAPA path ≤0.05 °C.

KAPA annealing suggestion. Roche publishes no Tm formula; the ReadyMix's higher salt shifts optimal Ta upward, and a gradient is recommended (65 °C fallback). The suggested Ta is the KAPA-condition NN Tm rounded to the nearest degree; the gradient brackets it.

Polymerase annealing guidance. NEB Q5 follows NEB's own Q5 PCR Kit manual (E0555): anneal 3 °C ABOVE the Tm of the lower-Tm primer, calculated under Q5 reaction conditions (2 mM Mg²⁺, 0.8 mM total dNTP, 500 nM primer, Owczarzy 2008) — not the SnapGene Tm. Following SnapGene/Thermo guidance for the other dsDNA-binding-domain enzymes: Phusion/Phire anneal 6–12 °C ABOVE the SnapGene primer Tm; Platinum SuperFi anneals 4–8 °C above; standard Taq anneals 0–5 °C below. The polymerase selector sets the suggested Ta and gradient window accordingly (Q5/SuperFi capped at 72 °C per NEB). For a primer pair, set Ta from the lower-Tm primer.

On matching SnapGene exactly. All "SantaLucia 2004" tools share identical NN stacking parameters (this tool matches an independent Biopython implementation to ≤0.06 °C). Small (±1 °C) differences between tools arise from the initiation-parameter version (Allawi/SantaLucia 1997 vs SantaLucia & Hicks 2004) and the salt-correction formula. This tool's raw SnapGene mode uses the 1997 initiation + 1998 salt correction.

SnapGene calibration (β). With the calibration checkbox on, the SnapGene Tm is empirically adjusted to match SnapGene's own output using TmSnapGene ≈ Tmraw − 1.0 + 3.19·(GC fraction), fit to 9 user-measured SnapGene values (TRAV/TRBV primers, 20–40 nt, ~60 °C; RMS 0.26 °C, reproducing all 9 after rounding to the nearest integer). The GC term reflects where SnapGene's salt handling diverges from ours. This correction is validated near the calibration range; treat it as approximate for very different lengths or Tm, and turn it off to see the physically raw value. Because SnapGene/NEB annealing guidance is defined relative to the SnapGene Tm, the calibration also feeds the Q5/Phusion/SuperFi/Taq annealing suggestions.

Limitations. Two-state model — hairpins, primer-dimers and secondary structure are not modeled (screen separately). Mapping assumes a perfectly matched 3′ annealing region; internal mismatches/bulges are not scored.
